# Marlo Quill (Quill and Kiln)

Marlo Quill makes small-batch stoneware in a shared Portland studio and sells it four times a year, plus a coffee-bar collaboration each autumn. About a third of what she sells comes straight off her own posts. The brand is the person: a real maker in a real room, warm and quiet, and never a polished label, because that is what her audience follows her for.

## Audience
Followers aged 22 to 34, the same age as Marlo, who buy 38 to 90 dollar stoneware and follow the maker rather than a brand.

## Positioning
A mid-tier creator retainer of 600 to 1,500 dollars a month, stoneware at 38 to 90 dollars. Above careless untreated phone photos, below gallery-ceramics gloss; a heavy uniform filter would read as selling out and cost her directly.

## Typography
A warm humanist serif with a hand feel for names and headings, paired with a simple unfussy sans for captions and prices. A maker's hand rather than an agency deck.

## Voice
First person, casual and direct. Example line: One look, mine, not a filter.

## Supplied Identity
No formal brand files. Her three motifs, a mug, a sprig and a small kiln, exist only as ink on a photographed sketchbook page she keeps re-cropping. The true glaze colours are pinned by a single reference frame of fired test tiles rather than any spec, and there is no logo file.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
