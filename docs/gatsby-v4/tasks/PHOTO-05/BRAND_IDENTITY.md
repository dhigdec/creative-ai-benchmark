# Halcyon Quarter

Halcyon Quarter is a fifty two apartment scheme by Kestrel and Vane on the old rope works site at Beckett Wharf. Phase one sells to professional downsizers leaving a family home nearby. What the scheme sells is the evening: warm rooms against a cool blue hour on the water, quiet and expensive rather than shiny, carried across the developer, the brokerage and the lender as one look.

## Audience
Professional downsizers aged 50 to 65 leaving a house they have owned for thirty years within twenty miles. Discerning, unhurried, distrustful of shiny marketing, and likely to count windows when they visit the site.

## Positioning
Premium off-plan residential, apartments from 780 thousand to 1.45 million dollars. Positioned as quiet and expensive, above bright price-led property marketing and apart from investor rental stock.

## Typography
A text serif with real hierarchy carrying headings and pricing, set plainly inside the copy, paired with a restrained sans for captions and specification. Quiet and expensive: generous margins, one photograph per canvas, no starbursts and no heavy price treatment.

## Voice
Calm, assured and understated. Brass not gold, the water cold and the rooms warm, nothing shiny. We state the address, the light and the offer without overselling. Example line: 'Fifty two apartments on the water at Beckett Wharf, releasing this autumn.'

## Supplied Identity
The Kestrel and Vane monogram exists only as sign paint, brushed by the sign writer onto the site hoarding board, with a single photograph of it and no artwork file. The lender's payment cards survive only as the flat file their printer ran last spring, with no working source held by anyone, and every figure has since changed.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
