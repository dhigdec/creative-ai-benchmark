# SlabVault Cards

SlabVault Cards is a small consignment house that lists and settles graded collectibles for private sellers. Lots run from roughly 150 to 5,000 dollars per item, and buyers decide from the photograph alone, zooming into the holder, the label and the corners before they bid. Consignors shoot their own slabs on their own tables, so the house exists to bring many different rooms to one credible standard. Trust is the entire product: everything SlabVault presents has to read as accurate, unretouched and verifiable.

## Audience
Condition-literate collectors aged 25 to 60 who buy graded cards and comics at 150 to 5,000 dollars a lot, read the grade and certification line straight out of the image, and walk away from anything that looks staged or concealed.

## Positioning
Mid to upper consignment. Not a bulk reseller or a toy shop, and not a fine-art auction room. The presentation sits deliberately between the two: neutral, generous and evidential, never cheap and flashy, never gilded and theatrical.

## Palette
- Slate Grey (primary ground): #8A9099
- Vault Charcoal (near-black text and frame): #1C1F24
- Paper Neutral (light stage): #F2F3F4
- Signal Amber (single restrained accent): #C8892E

## Typography
A quiet, legible pairing: a humanist sans for body copy and roster data, and a slightly firmer grotesque or restrained slab for lot numbers and headings. Type stays small, even and confident. No decorative or display faces.

## Voice
Plain, factual, collector to collector. States the grade, the grader and the condition without hype. Never breathless, never salesy. The photograph does the persuading.

## Supplied Identity
SlabVault has never held a proper brand kit. What exists is a house note describing the palette in words (grey, black, one amber accent already used on the printed stationery and the sleeve ticket), the grading partner names spelled the way the graders require, and the sale details. There is no logo file, no type specimen and no template, so the house look has to be established fresh and held consistently across the listings, the printed tickets and the auction-week set.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
