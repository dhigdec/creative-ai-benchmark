# Verranza Coastal Retreats

Verranza Coastal Retreats is the repeat client for this benchmark series. The canonical client context is Liguria, Italy, and this commission covers phase 2: booking collateral and in-villa season cards.

## Audience
Guests aged 40 to 65 booking a whole villa for a birthday, an anniversary or a multi-generation gathering, plus the travel agencies and private clients who book on their behalf. They have stayed in the real houses and will recognise anything that is not true.

## Positioning
Villas run 1,800 to 4,200 dollars a night depending on season, positioned against boutique hotels rather than other rentals. The grammar is generous space and quiet architecture, with rates carried in a discreet table rather than announced, so the collection never reads as an aggregator listing.

## Palette
- Verranza Stone: #D8C9B4
- Deep Olive: #4E5A43
- Coast Blue: #6E8CA0
- Warm Charcoal: #33302B
- Linen: #F3EEE4

## Typography
One quiet prestige system: an elegant high-contrast serif for display and villa names, set with air, paired with a clean humanist sans for amenity tables, the rate ladder and body. Restraint over flourish, so it reads considered without tipping into fashion-house coldness a family will not book from.

## Voice
Calm, warm, understated. Describes what the house actually is at that hour, never oversells. Example line: "Warm stone, a long terrace, and the pool in real afternoon light."

## Supplied Identity
The collection has a printed in-villa season card whose artwork is held by its printer as a press PDF, and a written owner note recording what the previous editor got wrong. There is no lost wordmark here; the constraint is photographic consistency across three villas shot on two trips by two people, with the second batch already worked on by an editor no longer used. The collection's one graphic mark is a small hand inked cove motif an artist drew for the founders, which survives only as a flatbed scan of the original inking and as the mark etched on Maison Aubine's door glass; it has never been redrawn or digitised, and the foil printer and the glass etcher need it as vector artwork. The contractual welcome provision left in every house exists only as a short still life set the manager shot on a villa's limestone counter.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
