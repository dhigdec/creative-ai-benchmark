# Redecker Machine Works

Redecker builds press brakes and metal forming presses, from forty thousand to three hundred thousand dollars a machine. The works has carried the same company mark since 1919. Our buyer reads the tolerance sheet before he reads anything we wrote, so everything we put a name on has to say precision.

## Audience
Procurement engineers and plant managers who buy on specification rather than feeling, across North America and northern Europe

## Positioning
Capital equipment at forty thousand to three hundred thousand dollars per machine. The mark is read as evidence about the machine: the register is flat, mechanical, high contrast and generously spaced, never heritage craft workshop and never consumer tech.

## Typography
A flat, mechanical grotesque for the mark and plate wording, set high contrast and widely spaced, paired with a plain industrial sans for the usage sheet and stationery. Nothing decorative, nothing hand drawn. Weight and spacing do the work.

## Voice
Spare, factual and confident; we state the specification and let it stand. No adjectives a tolerance sheet would not carry. Example line the brand would write: "Built to a hundred years of the same standard, plate by plate."

## Supplied Identity
We no longer hold usable artwork for the company mark in any form. The designer who last touched it retired in the nineties and nothing came back from his files. What survives is a flatbed scan of the 1974 parts catalogue cover, where the mark is printed in one deep ink on cream board, and a photograph a fitter took of the same mark etched into a stainless nameplate on a machine that came back for a rebuild. A works standards text file records the green as the paint shop mixes it, the address block and the required plate wording. Two further company marks also survive only as artefacts: the works proving stamp, held only as a die impression struck into a steel test coupon a fitter photographed, and the 1919 cast heritage device, held only as a raking light photograph of the badge cast and painted into a machine's main frame.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
