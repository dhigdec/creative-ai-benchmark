# Evergreen Provisions

Evergreen Provisions runs two dispensary shops in Bend, built around six house strain families. Two years ago the owners paid a local sign painter to hand-paint a crest panel for each family onto the shop walls. Customers photograph them constantly and ask where they can buy them, and the first apparel capsule exists to answer that question without losing the hand that made the panels.

## Audience
Craft-minded dispensary customers aged 25 to 45 who buy at the counter on the strength of the wall panels they already photograph. People who pay for the painted origin, not for a logo.

## Positioning
Tees at 32 dollars, hoodies at 70 dollars, short-run and counter-sold. Elevated streetwear priced above gas-station merch and below a designer drop. The painted texture is the product, so the value sits in the brush surviving, not in gloss or foil.

## Palette
- Aubergine Deep: #3B2B3A
- Cedar Bark: #6B4A2E
- Sage Field: #7C8A5F
- Bone Primer: #E7E0D0
- Bench Black: #1A1714

## Typography
Display in a painted or brushed-character face that echoes the sign painter's hand, kept legible. Support and spec type in a plain workhorse sans so the plates and sheets read cleanly. Type never competes with the painted crest.

## Voice
Warm, plain-spoken, shop-floor honest. No luxury posturing and no carnival hype. Example line: "These are hand painted panels and that is the entire reason anyone wants them. Keep the brush in it, and tell us plainly what came back and what did not."

## Supplied Identity
The six crest panels were hand-painted by a local sign painter who has since moved to Montana and left no sketches or files. The only surviving artwork is a set of bench photographs of the finished panels, shot over two visits under mixed light. There is no vector art and no separation art, which is exactly why the screen printer cannot quote a run.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
