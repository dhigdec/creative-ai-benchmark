# Ember and Oak

Ember and Oak is a wood fired grill on the west side of Providence, one hearth you can see from every table, mains around thirty four dollars. Launch week opens the second room. The brand is fire, char and appetite: warm, loud and alive, built to make somebody hungry in the first two seconds.

## Audience
Walk-in diners on the west side deciding in the time it takes to scroll past, plus delivery orders. Neighbourhood people who want a room that feels alive, not a tasting menu and not a value grill.

## Positioning
A casual premium wood fired grill, mains around thirty four dollars. The grade runs warm, the cut is fast, the food fills the frame and the type is bold and immediate. Never moody fine dining, never a discount grill with a deal burned across the frame.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Grounds and panels are Charcoal, Flame Yellow or Cream, and every closing card has one of them as a solid ground. Type and the mark are Cream on Charcoal and Charcoal on Flame Yellow, as type_system step 02 sets, and Charcoal on Cream, as emberoak_mark.png sets the mark; type set directly on footage is Cream. A price or room line sits only on a solid Charcoal or Flame Yellow panel (type_system step 02), never on Cream or directly on footage. Ember Orange is the hot bar behind every dish name and is never type, a ground or a panel. Beyond the hot bars, Ember Orange, Oak Brown and Char Red are accents for thin rules and dividers only, together no more than 5% of the designed, non-footage area.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, glow, shadow, outline or blend mode is applied to any palette colour, type, bar or the mark, and no scrim, vignette, gradient or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over footage or a still: type that needs separation from the footage sits on a solid panel in the colours the distribution rule sets. A colour grade applied to the footage itself is not an overlay. Cuts and dissolves between shots are transitions, not tints; colour and opacity are judged on a settled frame.
- Forbidden: No colour outside the six palette hexes for type, bars, panels, rules, buttons or icons, and no discount or deal flash, badge or burst in any colour, which the type rules forbid.
- Photography: Footage and stills keep their own colour under the warm grade the brand positioning sets. A warm colour grade is allowed; a flat palette-colour overlay, tint or duotone that replaces their own colours is not. No detail is painted, generated or extended into footage or stills.

## Typography
Display face: Red Hat Display Black. Text face: Atlas Grotesk.

- 01 Display / H1 · dish name: Red Hat Display Black, 112 px / 120 px, Black, capitals only, tracking minus 0.020 em.
- 02 Subheading / H2 · price and room line: Atlas Grotesk Bold, 56 px / 62 px, Bold, tracking minus 0.010 em.
- 03 Body · dish description: Atlas Grotesk Regular, 34 px / 44 px, Regular, tracking 0.
- 04 Fine print · hours, delivery, address: Atlas Grotesk Regular, 22 px / 30 px, Regular, tracking plus 0.030 em.

Signature move: The hot bar. Every dish name sets in Red Hat Display Black capitals over two lines at 112 px on 120 px, tracked minus 0.020 em, with a 12 px Ember Orange bar burning behind the baseline of the lower line and stopping flush with its final letter, so the name always looks like it is resting on something hot. That bar is the first thing a viewer sees.

Rules:
- Never set a dish name in lowercase.
- Every dish name breaks over two lines; the Ember Orange bar sits behind the baseline of the lower line only and stops flush with its last letter, never running past it.
- The bar is 12 px deep at the 112 px screen size and scales with the name, never with the frame.
- Dish names always track minus 0.020 em; no other step may borrow that tracking.
- In print, dish names set 34 pt and body 10 pt on 14 pt.
- Never let the type go moody fine dining, and never burn a discount deal across the frame.

## Voice
Warm, direct, hungry. Short lines with heat in them. Example line the brand would write: 'The chop, the char, the fire you can see from your table. Second room now open.'

## Supplied Identity
The mark survives only as a small raster: a two word lockup inside a thin ring, sitting on a cream square baked into the picture rather than behind it. It has to be recovered as clean, placeable artwork before it can go on the end card or the window poster.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
