# SlabVault Cards

SlabVault Cards is a small consignment house that lists and settles graded collectibles for private sellers. Lots run from roughly 150 to 5,000 dollars per item, and buyers decide from the photograph alone, zooming into the holder, the label and the corners before they bid. Consignors shoot their own slabs on their own tables, so the house exists to bring many different rooms to one credible standard. Trust is the entire product: everything SlabVault presents has to read as accurate, unretouched and verifiable.

## Audience
Condition-literate collectors aged 25 to 60 who buy graded cards and comics at 150 to 5,000 dollars a lot, read the grade and certification line straight out of the image, and walk away from anything that looks staged or concealed.

## Positioning
Mid to upper consignment. Not a bulk reseller or a toy shop, and not a fine-art auction room. The presentation sits deliberately between the two: neutral, generous and evidential, never cheap and flashy, never gilded and theatrical.

## Palette
- Slate Grey (primary ground): #8A9099 · mandatory. The primary ground: the tile background or the stage around the slab. Also small secondary labels on Paper Neutral, as the consignor and condition labels on slabvault_lot_card.pdf. Never the lot number, title, grade line, certification line or condition note.
- Vault Charcoal (near-black text and frame): #1C1F24 · mandatory. All primary type (lot number, title, grade line, certification line and condition note), set on Paper Neutral as on slabvault_lot_card.pdf, plus thin frames and keylines, as around that ticket and its thumbnail. Never a ground or panel.
- Paper Neutral (light stage): #F2F3F4 · mandatory where type sits. The ground under all type, as the ground of slabvault_lot_card.pdf, and optionally a light stage behind the slab. Never type.
- Signal Amber (single restrained accent): #C8892E · optional accent. The single restrained accent: at most one hairline rule or small marker per tile, as the one hairline under the masthead of slabvault_lot_card.pdf. Never type, a ground or a panel.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Grounds and panels are Slate Grey or Paper Neutral and nothing else. Slate Grey is the primary ground: the tile background or the stage around the slab. All type sits on Paper Neutral, as on slabvault_lot_card.pdf: the lot number, title, grade line, certification line and condition note are Vault Charcoal, and small secondary labels may be Slate Grey. Vault Charcoal also draws thin frames and keylines and is never a ground or panel. Signal Amber is the single accent: at most one hairline rule or small marker per tile, no more than 5% of the designed, non-photographic area, never type, a ground or a panel.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, shadow or blend mode is applied to any palette colour, and no scrim, gradient or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over a photograph. No drop shadow, glow or reflection is added to a slab: slabvault_house_note.txt says never add an edge, a shine or a shadow that was not there.
- Forbidden: No gold, bronze, copper, silver or other metallic effect on any type, ground, panel, rule, keyline or icon, and no bright or saturated colour there other than the single Signal Amber #C8892E accent: the house is never gilded and theatrical, never cheap and flashy. No accent colour other than Signal Amber, and no colour outside the four palette hexes (#8A9099, #1C1F24, #F2F3F4 and #C8892E) for type, grounds, panels, rules, keylines or icons.
- Photography: Photographs keep their own corrected colour: a neutral correction of the room's colour cast (warm bulb, lamp or cool bathroom light) is allowed, but the printed colours of the card or comic, the clear holder and the holder label are never recoloured toward the palette, and no palette colour is tinted into a photograph. This holds for the frame the output register binds to each tile (slabvault_consignment_2026F_01.jpg for LOT-001 through slabvault_consignment_2026F_14.jpg for LOT-014).

## Typography
Display face: Redaction (Regular and the dotted 35 cut). Text face: Söhne (Buch and Söhne Mono Regular).

- 01 Display / H1, lot number: Redaction 35, 44 px / 48 px, Regular, tracking -10 units.
- 02 Subheading / H2, lot title: Söhne Buch, 30 px / 34 px, Buch, tracking -5 units.
- 03 Body, grade line: Söhne Buch, 22 px / 32 px, Buch, tracking 0 units, max 65 characters.
- 04 Fine print, certification line: Söhne Mono Regular, 18 px / 26 px, Regular, tracking +20 units.

Signature move: The register print. Every lot number runs in Redaction 35, the visibly dotted cut, at 44 px on 48 px, the single degraded element on an otherwise crisp tile, so it looks struck by the vault's own ledger printer.

Rules:
- Never letterspace the grade.
- The dotted Redaction 35 cut is reserved for the lot number. Nothing else on the tile is ever set in it.
- Every lot number runs at 44 px on screen and 18 pt on the printed ticket, with the grade at 9 pt.
- The lot number is the first thing read: it always precedes the lot title in reading order.
- Apart from the lot number, everything on the tile stays crisp. No second degraded or distressed element.
- Certification digits are always set in Söhne Mono Regular, never in the Buch cut.

## Voice
Plain, factual, collector to collector. States the grade, the grader and the condition without hype. Never breathless, never salesy. The photograph does the persuading.

## Supplied Identity
SlabVault has never held a proper brand kit. What exists is a house note describing the palette in words (grey, black, one amber accent already used on the printed stationery and the sleeve ticket), the grading partner names spelled the way the graders require, and the sale details. There is no logo file, no type specimen and no template, so the house look has to be established fresh and held consistently across the listings, the printed tickets and the auction-week set.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
