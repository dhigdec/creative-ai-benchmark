# SB3-088-MOT | SentinelMesh - Product demo and narration package

SentinelMesh is going in front of security buyers and we need the product cut. Be aware of what the pack actually holds: every supplied clip runs five seconds and none of them carry audio, so a narrated demonstration is not on the table. What we want is a short product film that lets the real screen captures do the work, with the cleared claims set as on screen text and each claim card held for at least two seconds over the screen that supports it, so a security engineer can read the interface detail that matters.

Please prepare
- Product demo master: 1 named export, 12 to 15 seconds, 16:9, under the supplied instrumental bed (sentinelmesh_bed.wav). The screen state on show must support the claim on screen at that moment, the claim wording must come from the cleared rows of the messaging sheet, one claim card is the security engineer's line “Catches the risky moment before a person clicks.”, and the film closes on the booking action.

- Product website band and Website feature panel: not part of this round; deliver only the two cuts above.

House rules for this package
- No narration. The recorded read (sentinelmesh_vo.wav) stays out of these cuts, and no synthetic voice replaces it. The message is carried by on screen text.
- The approved closing action is set exactly as: Book a walkthrough. It is the last thing on screen on the master.
- Each cut shows the SentinelMesh shield mark at least once, on the Sentinel Navy ground, drawn as in the flat export of the mark on white.
- Each cut sets the product name “SentinelMesh” as type in the brand faces.
- Besides the product name and the closing action, only the Claim wording of rows marked Cleared in sentinelmesh_messaging.csv may appear on screen, word for word; the Proof Point column is background, not screen copy. The claim about cutting repeat risky behaviour by 60% is in legal review and must not appear anywhere.
- Precision over spectacle: no fear language, no exclamation, one idea per card.
- Video delivery means a playable MP4, not a storyboard, still montage PDF or editing log. Live action edits require adequate distinct footage: looping one short clip is not a substitute for the missing coverage.
