# The Marchfield Exchange

The Marchfield Exchange is a listed 1911 commercial building let to law, architecture and accountancy firms. The building itself, its stone entrance and brass fittings, is the reason tenants take space here. Its house crest is original to 1911, and the sign programme is being rebuilt as one coherent system that respects the building's character.

## Audience
Professional firms choosing a building with a stone entrance and brass fittings, and a building board approving the spend. Tenants who read signage as a signal of how the building is kept.

## Positioning
A listed heritage building letting at 45 to 75 dollars per square foot. The programme is engraved brass and stone appropriate metalwork, deep margins and generous clear space. Anything that would look at home on a retail park reads as neglect at this rent.

## Palette
- Marchfield Brass: #A9863F · mandatory in the Brass reproduction proof; optional elsewhere. The flat field that stands for satin brass behind the crest in the Brass reproduction proof, as marchfield_sign_survey.txt asks proofs to be judged on brass. It may also be the flat ground of the suite plate, entrance plaque and directory artwork, standing for the brass face, and it may be the one colour of crest-reversed.svg. Never type or a line, and never the crest outside crest-reversed.svg.
- Limestone: #D8CFBE · optional. A light colour for crest-reversed.svg, the crest drawn to sit on a dark ground, or the page ground in the Brass reproduction proof. Never engraved and never type on a light ground.
- Slate Ink: #2C3439 · mandatory. The one engraving ink, as on marchfield_suite_plate_2019.pdf: every engraved element of the suite plate, entrance plaque and directory artwork (crest, building name, MCMXI, suite numbers, tenant names), the single colour of crest-master.svg and engraving.svg, the crest on the brass field of the proof, and the proof's text, where only a flag label may be Oxblood instead.
- Oxblood: #5E2A2A · optional accent, Brass reproduction proof only. The marks and labels that flag a detail under the 0.50 mm stroke limit in the proof, where Slate Ink may be used instead. Never engraved, never a ground, never the crest.
- Facade Grey: #7C7468 · mandatory for the bolt-centre guide marks on the entrance plaque artwork; optional accent elsewhere. Guide marks that are never engraved: the bolt-centre marks and any margin or dimension lines on the artwork, and captions or dimension lines in the proof. Never the crest, the building name, a suite number or a tenant name.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Everything engraved, the crest, the building name, MCMXI, suite numbers and tenant names, is Slate Ink, the one engraving ink of marchfield_suite_plate_2019.pdf. On the suite plate, entrance plaque and directory artwork the ground is either left unprinted, as the white proof ground of that file, or flat Marchfield Brass for the brass face, and nothing else. crest-master.svg and engraving.svg are Slate Ink alone on a transparent ground; crest-reversed.svg is one single palette colour that reads on a dark ground, Limestone or Marchfield Brass. In the proof the crest sits in Slate Ink on a flat Marchfield Brass field, pages are unprinted or Limestone, and text is Slate Ink, except that a mark or label flagging a detail under the 0.50 mm limit may be Oxblood. Facade Grey is only for guide marks that are not engraved and Oxblood only for flags in the proof, together no more than 5% of any page.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, shadow, sheen, texture or blend mode is applied to any colour, and no scrim, gradient or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over a photograph.
- Forbidden: No gold foil, chrome, bronze or other metallic or reflective effect on any type, line, crest or ground: brass is shown only as flat Marchfield Brass. No colour outside the five palette hexes for type, lines, the crest, marks or grounds, apart from the unprinted white ground of the artwork, as on marchfield_suite_plate_2019.pdf.
- Photography: No photograph is placed in the crest exports, the engraving artwork, the suite plates, the entrance plaque or the directory artwork, which are vector engraving artwork. marchfield_plaque.jpg and marchfield_letterhead.jpg are tracing references only: no colour is sampled from them, and the brass in marchfield_plaque.jpg is shown as flat Marchfield Brass. Any view of marchfield_facade.jpg in the proof keeps its own colour and is not recoloured toward the palette.

## Typography
Display face: Scotch Modern Display Roman. Text face: Scotch Modern Roman.

- 01 Display / H1: Scotch Modern Display Roman, 96 pt / 104 pt, Roman (400), caps, tracking 0.16 em.
- 02 Subheading / H2: Scotch Modern Roman small caps, 28 pt / 40 pt, Roman (400), small caps, tracking 0.10 em.
- 03 Body: Scotch Modern Roman, 10 pt / 15 pt, Roman (400), tracking 0, max 65 characters.
- 04 Fine print: Sackers Gothic Medium, 9 pt / 12 pt, Medium, tracking 0.12 em.

Signature move: The crest, the building name and MCMXI in oldstyle figures at one quarter the cap height always stack on a single vertical axis, and the date is never dropped.

Rules:
- Never bold a tenant name.
- MCMXI is never dropped, and it always sets in Scotch Modern oldstyle figures at one quarter the name's cap height.
- Crest, name and date always centre on one single vertical axis.
- Scotch Modern Display Roman caps are always letterspaced 0.16 em, period correct to 1911.
- Deep margins and generous clear space throughout. Anything that would look at home on a retail park reads as neglect at this rent.
- The programme stays editable so the agent can update a tenant name without a designer.

## Voice
Formal, measured, understated. A line the agent would write: "The Marchfield Exchange. Established 1911. Professional suites in the heart of the city."

## Supplied Identity
No drawing, plate or digital file of the crest exists, and the original architect's practice closed in 1961. The crest survives in exactly two places: engraved on the weathered brass entry plaque, photographed on the wall, and printed on a 1950s tenant letterhead, scanned. The suite plates survive only as the 2019 sign shop's single press PDF.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
