# DAYDRIFT

DAYDRIFT is a slow, unhurried interview show built around one guest and one real conversation that runs the better part of an hour. It is made by two people in a room they know well, funded by a small paying membership and one renewing sponsor. The promise is intimacy: you are sitting in on a talk between people who like each other, not watching a broadcast.

## Audience
Regular listeners aged thirty to fifty, most of them listening at work through the day. About two hundred paying members at eight dollars a month, plus a few thousand free listeners who click through to the channel page.

## Positioning
Membership at eight dollars a month with one modest renewing sponsor. Squarely independent: above hobby, well below a network production. The look must read considered and hand made, never broadcast and never scrappy meme.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Grounds, cards and panels are Foam Cream or Deep Room Brown and nothing else. On Foam Cream, the episode title and the show name are Deep Room Brown, and the guest name, credits and the sponsor line are Slate Ink, as on daydrift_card_2025.pdf; a recording date may be Muted Sage. On Deep Room Brown, and wherever type sits directly over the lamplit footage, all type and the show name are Foam Cream. Lamplight Amber (hairline rules and picture-window keylines) and Muted Sage (a date line or small dividers) together cover no more than 5% of the designed, non-footage area and are never a ground, card, panel, title or guest name.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity, on the covers and on every held frame of the films. No tint, transparency, gradient, shadow, glow or blend mode is applied to any palette colour, and no translucent scrim, gradient or tinted overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over the footage or a frame taken from it: a text card sits over or beside the footage as a solid block. A straight cut or a plain fade between shots is a transition, not a tint.
- Forbidden: No colour outside the five palette hexes for type, grounds, cards, panels, rules, keylines or the show name, plain white and black included. No gradient, glow or drop shadow on any type, card or the show name, and the show name is never shown as a photograph of the foam flag.
- Photography: The supplied footage (daydrift_ep02.mp4, daydrift_ep02_room_02.mp4, daydrift_ep05.mp4, daydrift_ep05_table_02.mp4, daydrift_ep08.mp4 and daydrift_ep08_room_02.mp4) and any frame taken from it are never tinted, duotoned or graded toward the palette, Lamplight Amber included: the lamplit rooms are not warmed further to match it. A correction confined to the lamp side of daydrift_ep08.mp4 is allowed.

## Typography
Display face: Romie. Text face: Cronos Pro.

- 01 Display / H1: Romie, 64 px / 78 px, Regular, tracking -5 units.
- 02 Subheading / H2: Romie, 44 px / 52 px, Regular Italic, tracking -2 units.
- 03 Body: Cronos Pro, 34 px / 48 px, Light, tracking 0.
- 04 Fine print: Cronos Pro, 26 px / 36 px, Light, tracking +10 units.

Signature move: Italic is reserved for exactly one thing, the guest name in Romie Italic at 44 px directly under every episode title, the only slanted type anywhere in the show.

Rules:
- Never typeset the show name; it stays the flag artwork.
- Italic is reserved for the guest name alone; no other step, in any size, slants.
- The guest name sits directly under the episode title, never above it and never on the same line.
- Episode titles set at 72 px on trailers and 24 pt on 29 pt in print.
- Cronos Pro Light sets credits at 34 px, dates and the sponsor line at 26 px, and 9 pt in print.
- No step oversells a guest; the title states the subject and stops.

## Voice
Plain, warm and a little understated, the way two friends actually talk. We never oversell a guest. Example line the brand would write: "Episode eight. We sat down with her for an hour and did not want it to end."
