# OSTRA

OSTRA is a counter, not a dining room: guests sit around the pass for one seasonal tasting menu at 135 dollars a head. The room is near black, spare and unhurried, and the photography is meant to feel like walking into it rather than like an advertisement. One plate at a time, on a dark ground, with light that still reads as light.

## Audience
Guests aged 30 to 55 booking an occasion around six weeks ahead, who expect calm and consideration rather than volume.

## Positioning
A 135 dollar seasonal tasting counter. The grammar is a near black ground, one plate per frame, generous emptiness, restrained saturation, and speculars on sauce and glass that read as light rather than as blown white or flat grey. Never a bright, crowded bistro look, and never near monochrome art photography in which the food cannot be read.

## Palette
- Near Black: #0E0E0F · mandatory on the menu announcement and the reservation story. The page ground of the menu announcement and the reservation story, and the ground Bone type sits on. On the dish tiles it is used only for a band or panel behind the caption, if there is one.
- Slate Pass: #1C1E20 · optional ground. An alternative to Near Black for a band or panel behind type, matching the slate pass in the photographs. Never a type colour.
- Ash Grey: #5A5A5C · optional accent. Thin dividers or outlines; on a Bone ground only, small fine print, as the date line on ostra_menu_card_autumn.pdf. Never a ground, and never type on Near Black, Slate Pass or a photograph.
- Bone: #E6DFD2 · mandatory. The wordmark, the house mark reproduced from ostra_house_mark_plate.jpg as a flat mark, every course title and caption, every detail line, any fine print and the booking or reservation action. On the menu announcement and the reservation story it sits only on Near Black or Slate Pass; on the dish tiles the caption may also sit on the dark slate of the photograph.
- Subdued Brass: #9C8459 · mandatory where the wordmark appears. The 0.4 pt hairline under the O S T R A wordmark, cut to exactly the wordmark width, and at most a small label, as the pairing-flight label on ostra_menu_card_autumn.pdf. Never a ground, panel, button, wordmark, house mark or course title.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: The page ground of the menu announcement and the reservation story is Near Black, and Slate Pass may be a band or panel behind type. On the menu announcement and the reservation story, type sits only on Near Black or Slate Pass; on the dish tiles, the caption may sit on the photograph's own dark slate or on a Near Black or Slate Pass band. The wordmark, the house mark, course titles, captions, detail lines, fine print and the booking or reservation action are Bone, and no type sits over the plate or the food. Ash Grey is never type on these dark pieces, only thin dividers or outlines, and Subdued Brass is only the wordmark hairline or a small label; together they cover no more than 5% of the designed, non-photographic area and are never a ground.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, shadow, glow or blend mode is applied to any palette colour or to type, type carries no outline stroke, and no scrim, gradient or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over a photograph: a band behind type is a solid block. Every export is fully opaque, because the delivery platforms put every picture on a white card.
- Forbidden: No colour outside the five palette hexes for type, panels, rules, buttons or icons; the old press card's Warm-Ink #3A2A22 is not a palette colour. Subdued Brass is a flat colour, never a metallic, foil or gradient effect. No bright or saturated accent colour: never the bright, crowded bistro look.
- Photography: Photographs keep their own corrected colour and are never recoloured toward the palette, toned to near monochrome or pushed to bright saturation. The slate in the photographs keeps its own tone and texture and is not crushed or recoloured to the Near Black hex. The thirteen frames read as one room under one white balance. The one white balance is neutral: pale plates read neutral, neither green, cool nor candle-warm. Speculars on sauce and glass read as light, not as flat grey.

## Typography
Display face: Recife Display. Text face: Suisse Int'l.

- 01 Display / H1: Recife Display, 52 px / 56 px, Light, tracking minus 10 thousandths.
- 02 Subheading / H2: Recife Display, 40 px / 44 px, Light, tracking minus 5 thousandths.
- 03 Body: Suisse Int'l, 22 px / 32 px, Light, tracking 60 thousandths.
- 04 Fine print: Suisse Int'l, 16 px / 24 px, Light, tracking 80 thousandths.

Signature move: The wordmark is always O S T R A in Recife Display Light caps tracked 320 thousandths, open enough that the five letters nearly come apart, centred over a 0.4 pt Subdued Brass hairline cut to exactly the wordmark width. That extreme air between letters registers first.

Rules:
- Never set a course title in the sans.
- The wordmark is always O S T R A in Recife Display Light caps tracked 320 thousandths, centred over a 0.4 pt Subdued Brass hairline cut to exactly the wordmark width.
- The hairline is cut to the wordmark width exactly, never run wide.
- Course titles come spelled as the kitchen spells them and are never corrected.
- Detail lines stay Suisse Int'l Light, Bone on Near Black, tracked 60 thousandths.
- One plate per frame on a near black ground: type keeps its distance and never crowds the picture.
- Generous emptiness and restrained saturation, never a bright crowded bistro look.

## Voice
Spare and understated, the voice of a room that lets the plate speak. Example line the brand would write: "Five courses. One seating. Please arrive as the light goes."
