# Velvet Hour

Velvet Hour is a monthly ticketed jazz and folk night held in the ninety seat back room of a Providence bar. Two sets, no support, one camera in the same corner every month. The night sells intimacy and musicianship to people who read a lineup carefully, so everything we make should feel like a good room rather than a club or a concert hall.

## Audience
Lineup literate adults roughly thirty to sixty who pay eighteen to twenty five dollars to sit close and listen. Locals who follow the acts, not a party crowd.

## Positioning
Mid, intimate. An eighteen to twenty five dollar ticket. Never budget club, never black tie concert hall.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Back Room Green is the main ground for the poster, the film end card and any solid panel, as on velvet_hour_listing_card_printer.pdf. Lamp Cream is a ground only for a small-print strip, where all type is Warm Ink, as that card's footer. On Back Room Green and over footage, act names, the venue name, billing, door time, price and body are Lamp Cream; the date line may be Ember Amber and the tagline Dust Rose, as on the card. The mark is drawn in flat Lamp Cream on Back Room Green, as painted on velvet_hour_lampbox.jpg. Ember Amber and Dust Rose are accents for hairline rules, the date line and the tagline only, together no more than 5% of the designed, non-photographic area, never a ground, a panel or an act name.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, shadow, glow or blend mode is applied to any palette colour, any type or the mark, and no scrim, vignette, gradient or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over footage or a photograph: type sits straight on the footage or on a solid Back Room Green card or panel. In the films a card or line may cut or fade in and out as a whole; colour and opacity are judged on a settled frame.
- Forbidden: No gold, foil, metallic sheen, glow, drop shadow or other display gimmick on any type, rule, panel or the mark, as the brand's own typography note rules out (“No display gimmicks, no gold foil gestures.”); Ember Amber is always a flat colour. No colour outside the five palette hexes for type, grounds, panels, rules or the mark.
- Photography: Footage and photographs keep their own colour and are never tinted, duotoned or washed toward the palette. The six takes keep the warm lamp light and the green room as filmed. The act photographs keep natural skin: next_month_act_01.jpg is not pushed grey or orange, and the printed halftone dots in next_month_act_04.jpg are not resampled into a moire. A bar still such as velvet_hour_still_07.jpg keeps natural skin under its warm cast, not taken grey by a whole-frame neutral balance.

## Typography
Display face: Adobe Caslon Pro. Text face: National 2.

- 01 Display / H1: Adobe Caslon Pro, 96 pt / 104 pt, Regular 400, true small caps on the surname, tracking -5 units on the given name, 120 units on the small caps surname.
- 02 Subheading / H2: Adobe Caslon Pro, 48 pt / 54 pt, Regular 400, true small caps on the surname, tracking -3 units on the given name, 120 units on the small caps surname.
- 03 Body: National 2, 11 pt / 16 pt, Regular, tracking 0, max 65 characters.
- 04 Fine print: National 2, 8 pt / 12 pt, Regular, tracking +25 units.

Signature move: Every act name stacks on two lines, given name above surname, the surname in true Adobe Caslon Pro small caps tracked 120 units so the second line falls quieter than the first. The stack holds at 96 pt for the headliner, 48 pt for supports and 54 px on the film title card.

Rules:
- Never set an act name in the sans.
- Every act name stacks on two lines, given name above surname.
- The surname is always true Caslon small caps tracked 120 units. Never fake small caps by scaling capitals.
- National 2 Regular sets billing and dates, Medium sets the ticket line.
- No exclamation marks and no hype. Short sentences, and the act's face and the music do the work.

## Voice
Plain, affectionate, low key. Talks about the room and the players, never hypes. Short sentences, no exclamation, lets the act's face and the music do the work.

## Supplied Identity
Our mark, a moth over a lamp inside a hairline arc of small caps type, was made by the designer who set up the night, and the source file went with them when they left. All that survives is the mark painted in one cream ink on the dark green lamp box by the door, which is why it has to be rebuilt from that as real artwork. No brand typeface was ever formally chosen, so the type system is open for you to set.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
