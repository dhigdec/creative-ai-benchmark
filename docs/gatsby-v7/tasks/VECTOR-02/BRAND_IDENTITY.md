# Redecker Machine Works

Redecker builds press brakes and metal forming presses, from forty thousand to three hundred thousand dollars a machine. The works has carried the same company mark since 1919. Our buyer reads the tolerance sheet before he reads anything we wrote, so everything we put a name on has to say precision.

## Audience
Procurement engineers and plant managers who buy on specification rather than feeling, across North America and northern Europe

## Positioning
Capital equipment at forty thousand to three hundred thousand dollars per machine. The mark is read as evidence about the machine: the register is flat, mechanical, high contrast and generously spaced, never heritage craft workshop and never consumer tech.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Grounds are Works Green, Signal White or Cream Board and nothing else: the machine plate's field is Works Green, with no band or panel of any other colour on the plate; works-mark.svg has a transparent ground; on the service header, unprinted paper counts as the Signal White ground. In works-mark.svg, the full-colour master (variant 1, "Master (full colour)" in redecker_works_standards.txt), the mark and any wordmark set with it are Works Green; Machine Black is the one-colour stamp of variant 2, "One-colour (Machine Black) for the invoice stamp", not this file; Plate Steel appears there only as the 0.5 pt rule beneath a REDECKER wordmark; Cream Board and Signal White never. On Works Green, all type and the mark are Signal White, and the only other colour there is a Plate Steel or Signal White rule. On Signal White or Cream Board, type and the mark are Machine Black or Works Green. Works Green is not required on the service header. Plate Steel is used only for the 0.5 pt rule beneath the REDECKER wordmark and hairline rules outside the mark, never for type, the mark or a ground.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, pattern, shadow, filter or blend mode is applied to any palette colour on the machine plate, the service header or in works-mark.svg, and no scrim or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over anything.
- Forbidden: No colour outside the five palette hexes for type, grounds, rules, the mark or icons. No metallic, brushed-steel, chrome, gold or silver effect, and no texture, distressing, halftone dot, bevel, emboss or drop shadow: the register is flat, mechanical and high contrast, never heritage craft workshop and never consumer tech.
- Photography: The supplied photographs and the catalogue scan are recovery sources, not artwork: none is placed in a deliverable as the mark or in its place, and no palette colour is sampled from them. Works Green is HEX 33564A as the paint shop mixes it, not the painted casting in redecker_frame_casting_1919.jpg, and Cream Board is HEX E8E2D2, not the aged board of redecker_catalogue_1974_cover.tif.

## Typography
Display face: Sohne Breit Halbfett. Text face: Suisse Int'l Regular 400 (Suisse Int'l Mono Regular 400 for serials).

- 01 Display / H1 · plate wordmark: Sohne Breit Halbfett, capitals only, 14 pt / 15 pt, Halbfett, tracking plus 0.200 em.
- 02 Subheading / H2 · works line and field labels: Sohne Breit Halbfett, capitals only, 7 pt / 9 pt, Halbfett, tracking plus 0.200 em.
- 03 Body: Suisse Int'l Regular, 9.5 pt / 13 pt, Regular 400, tracking 0, max 65 characters.
- 04 Fine print · serials and plate values: Suisse Int'l Mono Regular, 7 pt / 10 pt, Regular 400, tracking plus 0.040 em.

Signature move: REDECKER is always set in capitals tracked open to 0.200 em with a 0.5 pt Plate Steel rule directly beneath it at exactly the wordmark width, and MACHINE WORKS below at half the cap height on the same tracking, so the gaps between the letters read before the letterforms do.

Rules:
- Never condense the mark.
- Never italicise the mark.
- REDECKER always sets in capitals at exactly 0.200 em tracking; the tracking is never reduced to fit a space.
- The rule beneath the wordmark is always 0.5 pt Plate Steel at exactly the wordmark width, no wider and no narrower.
- MACHINE WORKS always sits below at half the cap height of the wordmark, on the same 0.200 em tracking.
- Suisse Int'l Regular 400 sets stationery; Suisse Int'l Mono Regular 400 sets serials and nothing else sets them.
- The register stays flat, mechanical, high contrast and generously spaced: never heritage craft workshop, never consumer tech.
- No adjective a tolerance sheet would not carry.

## Voice
Spare, factual and confident; we state the specification and let it stand. No adjectives a tolerance sheet would not carry. Example line the brand would write: "Built to a hundred years of the same standard, plate by plate."

## Supplied Identity
We no longer hold usable artwork for the company mark in any form. The designer who last touched it retired in the nineties and nothing came back from his files. What survives is a flatbed scan of the 1974 parts catalogue cover, where the mark is printed in one deep ink on cream board, and a photograph a fitter took of the same mark etched into a stainless nameplate on a machine that came back for a rebuild. A works standards text file records the green as the paint shop mixes it, the address block and the required plate wording. Two further company marks also survive only as artefacts: the works proving stamp, held only as a die impression struck into a steel test coupon a fitter photographed, and the 1919 cast heritage device, held only as a raking light photograph of the badge cast and painted into a machine's main frame.

Production identity and cross-image continuity remain subject to the release asset checks.
