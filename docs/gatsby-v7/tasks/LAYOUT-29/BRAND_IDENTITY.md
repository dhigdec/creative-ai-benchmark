# Rivermeadow Trust

Rivermeadow protects the meadows, hollows and river bends of one valley by holding easements and caring for a handful of small preserves. Many of our donors granted the land we now steward, so the relationship is personal and long. We exist so the fields stay fields for the people who walk them.

## Audience
Local giving households aged 45 to 78, land literate, many of them easement grantors who know the fields by name. Gifts run from 25 dollars to 25,000, a broad community base plus a small leadership circle that carries most of the campaign.

## Positioning
A community land trust, not a national charity. Two registers live in one base: a warm community of small and mid gifts, and a leadership circle giving four and five figures. Every dollar should read as spent on the land, not on ourselves.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: Every letter sits on a Cream Stock ground; a receipt sits on Cream Stock or the unprinted white of the paper, as on rivermeadow_receipt.pdf. No colour forms any other ground, panel or band. Field Ink sets the salutation, letter paragraphs, amounts, addresses, gift counts, gift summary lines and statutory wording. River Slate sets the trust mark, letterhead wordmark, header rule and signatory's name. The donor household name is Field Ink or River Slate. Bronze Patina sets giving level names and nothing else. Meadow Green (tagline, small labels, the river of the trust mark) and Heron Grey (keylines around photographs) together cover no more than 5% of the designed, non-photographic area.
- Opacity: Every palette colour is used solid, at 100% opacity. No tint, transparency, gradient, shadow or blend mode is applied to any palette colour, and no scrim, gradient or overlay of any colour, black and white included, is laid over a photograph: a photograph sits in its frame with no fade into the ground.
- Forbidden: No colour outside the six palette hexes for type, rules, keylines, panels or the mark. No metallic, foil, gloss, emboss or bevel effect on any type, rule or mark, Bronze Patina included: every dollar should read as spent on the land, not on a national charity's treatment.
- Photography: Preserve photographs keep their own colour and are never tinted, duotoned or recoloured toward the palette. A frame may be straightened or cropped, but not recoloured.

## Typography
Display face: Chivo. Text face: Adobe Caslon Pro.

- 01 Display / H1: Chivo Light, 30 pt / 33 pt, Light, tracking -0.01 em.
- 02 Subheading / H2: Chivo Bold, 11 pt / 13 pt, Bold, capitals, tracking +0.18 em.
- 03 Body: Adobe Caslon Pro Regular, 11 pt / 15.5 pt, Regular, tracking 0, max 65 characters.
- 04 Fine print: Adobe Caslon Pro Regular, 8.5 pt / 12 pt, Regular, tracking +0.02 em.

Signature move: The donor household's name is the largest thing on the sheet, Chivo Light at 30 pt, standing alone above the salutation and never outsized by a heading, an amount or a giving level. Step 01 is therefore the ceiling for the whole scale: the 16 pt Caslon heading, the 12 pt receipt amount and the 11 pt giving level all sit beneath it.

Rules:
- Never let a heading outsize a donor name.
- The donor household name stands alone above the salutation at 30 pt and is the largest thing on the sheet.
- Giving level names set Chivo Bold capitals at 11 pt tracked 0.18 em in Bronze Patina.
- Letter headings set Adobe Caslon Pro Semibold at 16 pt.
- Receipt amounts set at 12 pt with tabular figures.
- Approved letter copy, giving level names and thresholds, and the statutory receipt wording are used exactly as written.
- Web sets the letter at 18 px on 28 px.
- Every dollar should read as spent on the land, not on ourselves: no treatment that looks like a national charity's.

## Voice
Plain, grateful and specific, the voice of a neighbour rather than an institution. It names the field, not the fund. Example: We kept forty more acres in hay this year because you asked us to.
