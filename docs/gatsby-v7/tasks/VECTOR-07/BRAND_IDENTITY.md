# Wharfside Folk Festival

Wharfside is a three night folk festival on the working harbour at Fairhaven, in its twentieth year. Everything it prints comes off a two person hand screen shop that has printed the festival since the beginning, on a press that runs four inks plus the colour of the paper. The visible, hand pulled look is the festival's signature, and people frame the posters every year.

## Audience
Festival goers and locals at thirty five to sixty dollar tickets, many of whom already own the last five years of posters and buy the new one as a twenty dollar framed keepsake.

## Positioning
Community festival pricing, tickets thirty five to sixty dollars and posters sold afterwards at twenty dollars. The slightly imperfect hand pulled screen look is the value, not a corner cut.

### Palette rules
- Distribution: On the poster and handbill the ground is the Kraft Paper itself, left unprinted and never flooded, and the inks are the ones wharfside_shop_note.txt says the shop is mixing. Type prints solid in one ink, on the Kraft Paper ground or on a solid of another ink, or reverses out of an ink to Kraft Paper at least 2 pt wide; venue, date and ticket copy on Kraft Paper is Deep Ink, as on wharfside_nightcard_2025.pdf. Rust Red carries the 4 pt rule beneath each headline act name, and beneath any two-line Sentinel Black line on the shirt. On the shirt the undyed cotton is the ground and no more than two of the shop's inks print, with no Kraft Paper. festival-mark.svg uses only the shop's inks or Kraft Paper, on a transparent ground.
- Opacity: Every ink prints solid, at 100%, or as a coarse halftone dot of that one ink, which is the only tint: roughly 20 to 25 lines per inch, picked against the size the piece actually prints at, as wharfside_shop_note.txt says, used only to carry the tonal washes of wharfside_artwork_master.png. No transparency, blend mode, gradient, drop shadow, fine screen or process rosette, and no overlay of any colour, black and white included, laid over the painting.
- Forbidden: No colour outside the palette hexes for type, rules, panels, icons, the mark or the painting's separations. No more inks than wharfside_shop_note.txt allows, white included, and no process colour build.
- Photography: wharfside_artwork_master.png is reproduced only by separating it into the shop's inks and the Kraft Paper ground, keeping its drawing and proportions (cropping allowed); it is not repainted, restyled, stretched or printed as a full-colour image. wharfside_wordmark_shed.jpg is the source the mark is recovered from, not artwork to place, and its weathered timber is not carried into the mark.

## Typography
Display face: Sentinel Black. Text face: Adobe Caslon Pro.

- 01 Display / H1: Sentinel Black, 96 pt / 82.5 pt, Black, tracking -10 units.
- 02 Subheading / H2: Trade Gothic Bold Condensed No. 20, 34 pt / 36 pt, Bold Condensed, tracking +10 units.
- 03 Body: Adobe Caslon Pro Regular, 11 pt / 15 pt, Regular, tracking 0 units · max 65 characters.
- 04 Fine print: Adobe Caslon Pro Regular, 9 pt / 12.5 pt, Regular, tracking +8 units.

Signature move: Every headline act name is Sentinel Black caps at 96 pt broken across exactly two lines however short the name, leading pulled to 0.86 of the size, which is 82.5 pt, so the lines nearly touch, over a 4 pt Rust Red rule. Step 01 carries it, and step 02 is deliberately single line so the break stays the act name's alone.

Rules:
- Never set the wordmark in Sentinel.
- Every headline act name breaks across exactly two lines, however short the name.
- Leading on step 01 stays at 0.86 of the size and is never opened up.
- A 4 pt Rust Red rule sits beneath the two-line act name.
- Shirt art is the same Sentinel Black caps setting at 40 pt, never another face.
- The supporting bill stays on one line so the two-line break belongs only to act names.
- All type prints from the four inks the hand screen shop runs plus the colour of the kraft paper. The hand pulled register is the look, not a fault to be corrected.

## Voice
Warm, plainspoken, of the place. Example line: Three nights of folk on the old harbour, twenty years and counting.

## Supplied Identity
The wordmark exists only as white paint on the weathered timber of the harbour shed, photographed from the quay, with no file and none ever made. The approved twentieth year illustration exists as a single flattened scan of a gouache and watercolour painting, no layers. The screen shop's own act card from last year is on file as the format.
