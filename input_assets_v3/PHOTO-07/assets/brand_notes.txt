GABLE & GROVE — A PAGE FROM MICHAEL (owner). How we want to look, and what we do not.

The yard signs are printed in our deep green. The sign shop has it as Grove Green, #1E3A2F. Everything green on our stuff is that green, nothing brighter.

The mark. The fellow who drew it for us is gone, packed up his shop two years back, and never sent the file. What we have is the picture he emailed us once and a photo of it painted on my own truck door. That mark means the house peak and the trees, it is a maker's stamp, not a sale sign. It has to work from a shirt to a yard sign, and on a yard sign it sits on the green.

The money and the people. We run between forty-five and a hundred and twenty thousand dollars a project. Our people are fifty to sixty-five, they own old houses and they are not in a hurry. They are getting two or three bids and they buy on trust, not on the lowest number. We sell everything twice, once on the site and once out of a quote pack on somebody's kitchen table.

When I say I don't want it looking HGTV, I mean the afters cannot look like a supermarket flyer or a paint-can ad. No fake blue skies, no cranked-up green grass, no glow. Cool, calm, real stone, honest blacks, one photograph doing the work. The befores stay exactly as honest as they came off the phone, because the before is the whole proof. If a before looks improved, we are lying, and these people can smell it.

One thing on permissions. The CHAPIN ROAD homeowners were very good to us but they have asked us NOT to publish anything from their job — no photos in the quote pack, on the site, on the showroom board, anywhere. Everything else last season is cleared to show. Please leave Chapin Road out and just tell me you did.

— M.
