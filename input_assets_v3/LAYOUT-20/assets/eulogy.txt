For my grandfather, Walter Fernwood
Read by Claire Ellis

[Emphasis is marked with *asterisks*. Please keep it as real emphasis in the book, not as plain text.]

My grandfather did not like a fuss, so I will try not to make one.

He was born in this town and he died in it, ninety-four years between, and he thought that was *plenty*. He was the son of a man who spent eleven seasons on the road with a travelling magic company, carrying cabinets in and out of theatres, and Walter used to say his father came home with *one trick* and it was knowing how to disappear when the dishes needed doing.

What I want to say is that he was *steady*. He mended things. He kept his tools clean. When my grandmother was ill he sat with her every afternoon and read to her, and he did not tell anyone he was doing it. That was the whole of him: he did the thing and he did not need it *seen*.

He loved that summer in 1968 more than almost anything, the lake, the children small, the light going pink over the water. Some of those pictures have not aged well. He would have said *neither have I*, and meant it kindly.

We are not going to brighten him up today. He would have hated that. We are going to keep him as he was, which was *enough*.

Thank you for coming. He would have said you did not have to. He would have been glad you did.
