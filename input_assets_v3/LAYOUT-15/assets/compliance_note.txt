ALDERVALE MUTUAL - Compliance desk note for the producer kit
From: Eleanor Prewitt, Compliance Officer

What we will and will not sign, plainly.

WHAT COUNTS AS AN ADVERTISEMENT. Anything that reads as a sales pitch rather than a plain statement of fact. No superlatives, no urgency, no exclamation, no burst or starburst callouts, no implied guarantee of a return or of approval, no comparison that flatters us against a competitor. If a reasonable buyer at a kitchen table would feel sold to rather than informed, it does not go out. We are a ninety-one year old mutual; we look like one.

PRODUCERS ON PUBLISHED MATERIAL. Only a currently appointed, active producer may appear on a counter card or in any client-facing piece. If a producer's appointment has ended - resigned, terminated, lapsed - that producer must be removed before anything prints, regardless of what the licensing export still shows. The export lags; you check the status.

DISCLOSURES. The disclosure on a card or a client piece must appear exactly as written in the roster, word for word, and in full. A disclosure that is cut off, shortened, or stops before the sentence ends is a compliance failure and a regulatory exposure, not a design choice, even if the card looks fine otherwise. Producers licensed in more than one state carry that state's required language in full; it is longer, and it does not get trimmed to fit.

PHOTOGRAPHS. We may use a photograph of an appointed producer. We may NOT use a photograph of a policyholder, a client, or a real family, and we own none. If a piece needs an opening image that is not one of our own people, it must be a clearly licensed stock or illustrative image and it must be honest about being that.

RATES AND PLANS. When we show what a premium buys, the figures must be true to the pricing desk's table. Our three plans are not quoted the same way and must not be presented as one comparable set of numbers without a clear, plainly worded qualification that a family can read and understand. Do not put three differently based figures on one axis as though they were the same thing.

VOICE. Sober, plain, reassuring. A whole life policy is a promise that holds its value; say it like that. Nothing that sounds like a wealth manager and nothing that sounds like a mailer.
