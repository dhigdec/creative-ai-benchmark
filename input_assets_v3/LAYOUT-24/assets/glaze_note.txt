MARLOW GLAZES — a note from the studio, in plain words (no recipes here)

There are three glazes in the Marlow range this season and they are the whole point of it, so a word on each so you know what you are looking at.

OATMEAL is the reactive one. It breaks pale, almost bone, over any edge or throwing ridge where it runs thin, and it pools a soft warm brown down in the rings at the bottom of a bowl where it gathers. No two pieces pool exactly alike. It should read warm and matte-soft, not glossy, and the pale broken rim is part of it — do not lose it.

CELADON is the gloss one. Glassy, green-grey, a touch bluer where it lies thick. It is the one that shows everything: every highlight, every reflection of the light, and the bright line that runs along a rim is the glaze doing exactly what it should. It has to stay green-grey and must not be pushed blue, and the piece must keep its rim.

SLATE is the matte one. A dead flat dark grey-green that drinks the light and gives almost nothing back. No sheen at all. It photographs like a shadow if you are not careful, so it needs its own handling, and it must stay matte — the moment it looks wet or sheened it stops being slate.

They have to tell themselves apart on the page. A buyer orders by the glaze and receives a carton, so if the picture cannot separate an oatmeal from a slate we are the ones who hear about it. — G.
