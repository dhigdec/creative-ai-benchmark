ALDERWOOD SCHOLARS FUND -- TWENTIETH WINTER APPEAL (approved copy, do not edit)
The honour roll below cannot lose a single name.

=== APPEAL LANGUAGE ===
For twenty years, Alderwood Scholars Fund has run after-school learning centres in greater Providence and a small scholarship fund. This winter marks our twentieth year. Last year, ninety students finished the programme, and this appeal is where roughly half of our giving comes from.
We show our numbers plainly and we name where every dollar went, because our donors trust that the money reaches the students rather than the overhead. This year we opened our fifth centre, Cranston Fields, last September, so its first figures cover the months since it opened rather than a full year. Everything else covers the full programme year.
Twenty years is a milestone about the students, not about us. Thank you for being part of it.

=== THE ASK (for the donor deck's closing) ===
Our average gift is $45, and it matters. Our major asks this winter run from $5,000 to $50,000.
Closing line (major-donor meeting): "Will you consider a leadership gift of $50,000 to carry all five centres through the twentieth year?"
Closing line (general): "A gift of $5,000 funds a full year of tutoring for a centre's reading group. Will you join us?"

=== THREE CENTRE STORIES ===
WATERMAN ROW. When Priya took over the reading group at Waterman Row, eleven of her thirty-four students were reading below grade level. By spring, most had gained more than a full grade. "They stopped hiding the book under the table," she says. "They started asking to read out loud."

OLNEYVILLE GREEN. Olneyville Green runs the math group, and this year twenty-five of twenty-nine students finished. Danielle keeps a jar of worn-out pencils on her desk. "Every pencil in there is somebody's homework," she says. "That jar is the programme."

PAWTUCKET MILLS. Pawtucket Mills holds a family reading night in the old mill room under the painted tree. Parents read beside their children. "We didn't build it for the parents," Rosa says, "but they're the ones who cried."
